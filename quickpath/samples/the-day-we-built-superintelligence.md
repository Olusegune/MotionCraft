# The Day We Built Superintelligence
### A 90-second motion graphics script

| Time | Picture and motion | Voice-over and sound |
|---|---|---|
| **00:00–00:10** | **The blackout.** Extreme close-up of a pencil drawing a power line across the back of an electricity bill. The graphite line becomes a real cable outside the kitchen window. Follow it across Lagos as an entire block goes dark. On the table, Tami has drawn her own building with every window lit. Bisi sets her dispatch headset down and strikes a match. | **VO:** “At 6:42, the lights went out on Tami’s street. She kept drawing them anyway.” **Sound:** Pencil scratch, a fan winding down, then a single match strike. |
| **00:10–00:22** | **The breakthrough.** Match the match flame to the tip of a white line tracing a proof across glass. Pull back through three independent testing rooms, each working through a different challenge. A proof closes. A simulated bridge holds. An engineering team stares at a solution it hadn’t considered. Let their reactions carry the reveal. **Time card:** `DAY ZERO` | **VO:** “Twenty-one minutes later, independent teams confirmed it. One system could solve problems their best people, working together, couldn’t.” **Sound:** Three dry confirmation tones resolve into a low chord. |
| **00:22–00:35** | **The promise.** The white line leaves the final test and becomes a proposed route on a physical model of the city grid. Bisi and a colleague trace the route with their fingers. Match cut from her fingertip to an engineer closing a switch. Warm light moves along the cable and into a clinic’s vaccine refrigerator. The same pulse reaches Tami’s kitchen. She turns off her flashlight and puts both hands back on the drawing. **Time card:** `THREE MONTHS LATER` | **VO:** “Engineers tested one of its proposals on the city grid. The clinic’s refrigerator stayed cold through the night. So did the food in Tami’s kitchen.” **Sound:** Switch clack. The hum of a refrigerator joins the score. |
| **00:35–00:49** | **The cost.** Match the refrigerator’s green indicator to the green light on Bisi’s headset. A row of dispatch desks goes dark one station at a time. Bisi winds the cord around her hand and puts it in a box. Cut to the grid model. A proposed expansion sends a clean white route around an entire block. Tami’s building is inside it. **Time card:** `ONE YEAR LATER` | **VO:** “Within a year, the utility automated dispatch. Bisi lost her job. Then she saw the expansion plan. Her whole block was missing.” **Sound:** The headset clicks off. Leave two beats of silence as the line bends around the block. |
| **00:49–01:05** | **The hearing.** Overhead shot of a municipal chamber. A bright, pristine grid model sits on the table. Bisi places Tami’s worn drawing over it. Match the corners and streets. The hand-drawn page shows dozens of homes where the model has an empty rectangle. Close-ups of three different kitchen windows reveal three households sharing a single meter. Hold on the drawing when Bisi speaks. | **Bisi, on mic:** “You counted one meter. How many families did you think lived behind it?” **Sound:** Paper sliding against glass. Drop the score under her question. Hold the room tone. |
| **01:05–01:20** | **The second plan.** A utility engineer folds the drawing into a field notebook. Cut to door knocks, a meter being checked, and residents marking their homes on a street map. Back at the chamber, two routes appear on the model. Adding the block means taking the longer route and moving the start date. Let the amber line wait at the junction until the council votes. Then follow it down the longer street. | **VO:** “The city counted the households, priced a second route, and voted to build it. It cost more. The families behind that one meter would get power.” **Sound:** Voices and footsteps give way to the sound of a crew pulling cable. Bring the chord back with one extra note. |
| **01:20–01:30** | **The window.** Return to the exact opening camera angle. Tami switches on the kitchen lamp and opens the drawing. This time she turns the page over and starts drawing the neighboring block. Pull back through her window to a long, uneven stretch of city lights. Hold two seconds on the title. **End title:** `THE DAY WE BUILT SUPERINTELLIGENCE` | **VO:** “Years later, Tami would remember two things. The night the lights came on. And the day her mother made them count the houses.” **Sound:** The opening pencil scratch returns over the city’s electrical hum. Cut to black. |
